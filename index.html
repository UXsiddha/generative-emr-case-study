Generative EMR

A generative layer for Bajaj Finserv Health’s EMR. Doctors speak, and the form writes itself.

Role
Led the project
Timeline
3 months, 2025–2026
Team
2 product designers, 3 product managers
Status
Live

01Overview

What if the EMR could listen to the doctor, and fill itself in?

Traditional EMRs make clinicians enter structured data into rigid fields, during or after the visit.

The Generative EMR is a layer inside Bajaj Finserv Health’s existing record. Doctors type or speak in natural language, and the system writes the structured record in real time. It changes nothing about the record, and everything about filling it in.

05Key challenges

The goal was not to redesign the system, but to evolve it without disruption.

Three constraints, held at once: technical, behavioural, philosophical.

  1. 01

    An existing technical system

    Not greenfield. Backend, data models and workflows already in production, built in-house, so a drawing that could not be built was found out immediately.

  2. 02

    High-intensity users

    Doctors work under time pressure and resist anything that disturbs a known workflow. Small friction points cause outright rejection, not workarounds.

  3. 03

    A core that had to stay put

    Improve the record without changing what the record is. Rewriting the structure would solve a problem nobody had, and create one everybody would feel.

The constraint was agreed before any design decision was made. It ruled out the ambitious version of the idea in advance, and gave the team something to fail its own work against later. Which is exactly what happened.

06Approach

Reduce ambiguity early. Validate feasibility fast. Decide from working prototypes.

  1. Hypothesis

    AI can reduce documentation effort.

  2. Map

    The existing fields and workflows, and every moment a doctor repeated information or switched context.

  3. Bound

    Those moments, translated into clear, bounded AI use cases.

Three use cases

  1. 01Auto-prefill structured fields from typed or spoken input.
  2. 02Detect when a new symptom or diagnosis needs a new record component.
  3. 03Read a doctor’s history and background to produce better templates.

  3. Stage 3 Dropped

10The pivot

The version that demoed best was the one we dropped.

The first answer was a separate, chat-first application on the same database, with navigation tabs echoing the record’s original left panel.

Whiteboarded, then drawn as a full journey covering patient overview, detailed record and consultation start. It looked like the confident version of the idea.

One problem

A new chat-first platformStops at the constraint

A layer inside the record that already existsLive

It partially conflicted with our original principle of improving the system without altering its core, and raised legitimate questions around feasibility.

Note what that does not say. Not that it was wrong, or that users rejected it. It was judged insufficiently aligned with a principle committed to in advance.

11Generative EMR

The record stays. The typing goes.

The kept direction drops the new application entirely. The record stays where it was; an assistant docks alongside, and the doctor never opens a field to fill one.

Everything underneath is untouched: same tabs, same medication table, same examinations row, same footer actions. The core is not modified, it is supplied.

Voice and chat input

Doctors record the session. The EMR fills itself from the transcript.

A waveform with scrub and playback, not a record button. The doctor can hear what was said, which matters when the system has written into a clinical record on the strength of it.

Field-by-field readback

The assistant says what it wrote, instead of silently populating the form.

Smart fill goes further: components the doctor never mentioned get added to the record too.

A working surface, not a chat window

Slash commands for appointments, an inline calendar, quick actions in-thread, and hover-to-hide to reclaim the width.

12Personalisation

Could the record arrive already shaped for the person opening it?

An onboarding flow reads a clinician’s background out of the company’s existing doctor database, and generates a personalised, fully editable starting template.

Tested against four doctors’ data. The record already shipped with templates for Cough, Cold and Fever, chosen manually from a row of chips. This is that mechanism, with the choosing removed.

A cardiologist opens to

Cardiac risk profile
Smoking history, family history of CAD, dyslipidemia.

The assistant says so. Declared, not silent, and editable by chat, voice or hand.

13Final product

One consultation, start to finish, in one thread.

The whole journey happens in the panel, alongside a record the doctor never opens.

Designed
Session start with named capabilities, voice capture with scrub and playback, field-by-field readback, scheduling in-thread, and an end-of-consultation summary and handoff.
Decision
Nothing requires leaving the conversation. Capture, scheduling, summary and handoff all happen in one thread.
Achieved
A consultation is documented, scheduled and shared from a single thread.

  1. 01Session opens

  2. 02Doctor speaks

  3. 03Record fills

  4. 04Consultation ends

Reviewed before use

All outputs are intended to assist and should be reviewed before use.

One line sits in the footer of every screen carrying the assistant, permanently. It only means something if machine output is distinguishable from what a human entered, which is why the assistant reads back what it wrote, field by field. The confirmation step is not friction; it is the product taking its own disclaimer seriously.

14Where it stands

The feature is live.

Getting there took longer than the design did.

The prototype ran against an existing database, but connecting it to the company’s full-scale doctor database needed engineering expertise the team did not hold. For a stretch the feature was finished and unshippable.

The measures that matter

  1. What share of prescriptions get generated through conversation rather than typing.
  2. Whether the time from patient to completed record actually falls.

15Key learnings

Three things this project settled about designing for AI.

  1. 01

    Intelligence cannot be drawn, only run

    A static screen can claim the system understood. It cannot show it understanding. On AI work the prototype is the design, not a picture of it.

  2. 02

    The shift was structural, not cosmetic

    The gain came from changing how information gets into the record, not from redrawing it. Every screen we left alone was part of the result.

  3. 03

    Write the constraint down before the work

    One sentence, agreed in advance, is what let us kill our own best-looking direction. A principle only works if it exists before the thing it has to judge.
